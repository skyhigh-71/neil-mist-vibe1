Cut it tight

Most videos die in the edit. Here's how to finish them.

The routine

  1. Back up, then sort. Folders by angle or by section of trail.
  2. Pick the best clips only. Scrub fast. If you hesitate, it's out.
  3. Choose the track first. Cutting to the beat is far easier than fitting music afterwards.
  4. Rough cut. Clips in story order, no polish.
  5. Tighten. Trim every clip to the part that matters.
  6. Polish. Speed ramps, slow-mo, colour, sound effects, text.
  7. Watch it on your phone before you post it.

Pacing

  • Start on a strong clip, even if it happened mid-ride.
  • Fast riding: one to three seconds per cut.
  • Scenery and slow-mo can hang for three to six.
  • Change the angle every cut. Never show the same view twice in a row.
  • Cut on movement: into the corner, as the wheels leave the ground.

Music

Use tracks you have the rights to. A copyright claim can demonetise or pull the video. Licensed libraries and subscription services are the safe route, but read the licence. Keep music under any talking, add real trail noise quietly (tyres, gravel, chain) and let the track swell for the descent.

Colour

Fix exposure and white balance first. Match clips from the same ride so the greens agree. A touch more contrast and a little saturation is usually enough. Heavy filters date fast.

Shoot once, post four times

Frame with the middle of the shot in mind. A 4K landscape clip crops into a vertical cut without a reshoot.

Software

Pick one editor and learn it properly. Free ones can do pro work, and your phone's built-in editor is fine for a quick post from the car park.
