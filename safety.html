Stay rideable

The best content comes from people who keep riding.

The one rule

If you wouldn't ride it without a camera, don't ride it with one. A clip lasts a day. An injury can last a lot longer.

Riding with a camera

  • Helmet and protection every ride, whatever the shot.
  • Hit record before you roll off. Don't fiddle with it while moving.
  • Check every mount before every descent.
  • Keep heavy kit off your back if it throws your balance.
  • Build up to jumps and drops over many sessions. Don't debut them on camera.
  • Ride with someone, tell someone your route, carry first aid and a phone.

Filming other people

  • Ask before posting clips where someone is clearly identifiable.
  • Don't film children without a parent or guardian saying yes.
  • Blur or remove footage if someone asks.
  • If someone's hurt, put the camera down and help.

Trail manners

  • Stick to open, marked trails and respect closures, especially in the wet.
  • Give way to walkers, horse riders and uphill riders as local rules say.
  • Never park a tripod where it blocks the line.
  • Take your rubbish. Don't widen or damage trail for a shot.
  • Say thanks to the trail crew.

Drones and the law

Rules vary by country, region and landowner, and a lot of parks and trail networks ban drones or require a permit for commercial filming.

  • Read your aviation authority's current drone rules before you fly.
  • Check what the land manager allows.
  • Keep it in sight and away from people, animals and other riders.
  • Carry any registration or licence your country requires.

Before you go

Check the forecast, daylight and trail status. Pack water, food, layers, a repair kit and a light for the way home. Keep the camera dry and the batteries out of extreme heat or cold.
