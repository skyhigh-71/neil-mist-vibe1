Safety & etiquette

The best content comes from riders who keep riding. Film in a way that protects you, others and the trails.

The golden rule Never attempt something you would not ride without a camera. The audience will not remember a clip as long as you will remember an injury.

Riding while filming

  • Wear a helmet and appropriate protection every ride, regardless of the shot.
  • Do not look at the camera or fiddle with it while moving. Start recording before you set off.
  • Check mounts before every descent. A loose mount can fall into your wheel.
  • Do not carry a heavy kit on your back if it affects balance.
  • Ride within your ability on filmed features. Build up to jumps and drops over many sessions.
  • Ride with a friend, tell someone your route, and carry basic first aid and a phone.

Filming other people

  • Ask riders before you post footage in which they are clearly identifiable.
  • Do not film children without a parent or guardian's permission.
  • Be ready to blur faces or remove a clip if someone asks.
  • Never film a crash or injury in place of helping.

Trail etiquette

  • Stay on marked, open trails and respect closures, especially in wet conditions.
  • Yield to walkers, horse riders and uphill riders as local rules require.
  • Do not set up camera gear where it blocks a trail.
  • Take all rubbish with you, and avoid widening or damaging a trail for a shot.
  • Thank trail builders. They often work unpaid.

Drones, permits and the law

Rules differ by country, region and land manager. Many parks and trail networks restrict or ban drones and commercial filming.

  • Check the national aviation authority's current drone rules before you fly.
  • Read the land manager's rules on drones, permits and commercial filming.
  • Keep the drone in sight, away from people, wildlife and other trail users.
  • Carry any registration or licence that your country requires.

Weather and preparation

  • Check the forecast, daylight hours and trail conditions before you go.
  • Pack water, food, layers, a repair kit and a light for the way home.
  • Keep your camera dry and protect batteries from extreme cold or heat.

This page offers general guidance, not legal or professional advice. Local laws and trail rules always take priority.
