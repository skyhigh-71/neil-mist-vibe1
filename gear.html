The kit

You probably need less than you think. Here's what actually matters.

A cheap camera on a solid mount beats an expensive one swinging off your helmet. Sort the mount first, the camera second.

Pick your level

Models turn over every year. Read recent reviews and weigh stabilisation, battery and mounts over the biggest resolution number.

Mounts

  • Chest: the workhorse. Bars, hands and trail in one frame, and it stays steady.
  • Helmet: follows your eyes, so it feels natural. Top or front only, never the side.
  • Handlebar or stem: low and fast, with the front wheel in shot. Recheck the clamp, vibration loosens it.
  • Seatpost or frame: looks backwards down the trail. Cheap, and instantly different.
  • Tripod or pole: the one that changes your edits most. You, riding through a frame.
Do this

Use a tether on anything mounted. A camera that lets go on a descent is a hazard for you and everyone behind you.

Sound

Wind wrecks more clips than shake does. Wind-reduction on, a foam cover if the camera takes one, and plan to put music over riding. Talking bits? Record them standing still, away from the breeze.

Power and storage

  • Spare battery. Cold kills batteries, so keep it in an inside pocket.
  • Fast cards from a proper seller. 4K needs sustained write speed, and fakes are everywhere.
  • Back up the same day, to two places, before you wipe anything.
  • A microfibre cloth. Mud on the lens is the most common reason footage looks soft.

What to buy, in order

  1. A solid chest or helmet mount for the camera you own.
  2. A spare battery and a reliable card.
  3. A small tripod for trail-side shots.
  4. An action cam, if you started on a phone.
  5. Audio gear, then a drone. Last.
