Mounts: where your camera lives

Chest mount

The classic. Shows your hands, bars and the trail ahead. Stable and flattering, with a clear sense of speed.

Helmet mount

Looks where you look, which feels natural. Mount it on top or on the front, never on a side that could catch in a crash.

Handlebar / stem mount

Great for a low, fast-feeling view of the front wheel. Check clamp tightness often; vibration works things loose.

Frame / seatpost mount

Rear-facing shots of the trail disappearing behind you. Easy cinematic variety for very little effort.

Pole or tripod

For trail-side shots of you riding past or through a feature. A cheap compact tripod changes your whole style.

Safety tip Use a quality mount with a tether or safety leash, and keep cameras clear of your head's impact zones. A mount that breaks off can become a hazard.

Audio

Wind noise ruins more clips than shaky footage does. Plan your sound before you ride.

  • Use the camera's wind-reduction setting, or a foam windscreen (often called a dead cat).
  • Record voiceover afterwards for tutorials, instead of fighting wind on the trail.
  • A small wireless mic clipped near your collar helps for talking-to-camera segments at rest stops.
  • Plan to cover wind noise with music for pure riding clips.

Power and storage

  • Spare batteries. Cold weather cuts battery life sharply; keep spares in an inside pocket.
  • Fast, high-capacity cards. 4K recording needs cards rated for sustained write speeds. Buy from reputable sellers to avoid fakes.
  • Back up the same day. Copy footage to two places before clearing a card.
  • Lens cloth. Mud and fingerprints are the number-one cause of soft footage.

The shopping order that makes sense

  1. A solid chest or helmet mount for the camera you already own.
  2. A spare battery and a reliable memory card.
  3. A compact tripod for trail-side shots.
  4. An action camera if you started on a phone.
  5. Audio and, only later, a drone.

Prices and models change quickly. Check current reviews before you buy.
