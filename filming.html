Get the shot

Settings, angles and the habits that give you footage worth cutting.

Settings to start with

SettingTryWhy
Normal speed4K at 30 or 60 fpsSharp, and you can crop in later
Slow-mo1080p at 120 or 240 fpsJumps and berms look huge slowed down
ShutterAbout double the frame rateNatural blur. Faster looks choppy
StabilisationOn, highest settingSoaks up trail chatter
ColourStandard, or flat if you gradeFlat holds more detail but needs work

Menus differ camera to camera. Test before the big ride, not on it.

Angles

  • POV (chest or helmet): puts the viewer on the trail. Great, but not for every second.
  • Static wide: camera low on a tripod, you ride through. Gives scale.
  • Chase: follow a mate and let the line open up in front of both of you.
  • Details: tyres in dust, a drivetrain, a hand on the lever. Quick, and they make an edit feel rich.
  • Aerial: reveals the landscape. Only where it's legal and safe.

Light

Early morning and late afternoon give soft, warm light. Dust and spray look great backlit by a low sun. Dappled forest at midday confuses exposure, so find a gap or accept the contrast. Overcast is underrated: even light, rich greens, no harsh shadows.

Habits that save the edit

  • Shoot every feature twice with a different angle each time.
  • Hold static shots for ten seconds. You can always trim.
  • Film the run-in and the reaction, not just the feature.
  • Say the trail name and date out loud so you can find the clip later.
  • Wipe the lens before every run.

Ride-day shot list

Story shape

Where are we and why (setup), the climb or approach (build), the descent (payoff), then people and cooldown (ending). Even sixty seconds works better with that order.
