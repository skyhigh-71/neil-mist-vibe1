Filming guide

Settings, angles and habits that turn a ride into footage you can build a story from.

Camera settings that work

SettingRecommendationWhy
Resolution & frame rate4K at 30 or 60 fps for normal speedPlenty of detail, and room to crop or reframe in editing
Slow motion1080p at 120 or 240 fps for jumps and bermsSmooth slow motion; plan to slow it to 24 or 30 fps in the edit
Shutter speedAbout double your frame rate, e.g. 1/120 at 60 fpsNatural motion blur; faster shutter looks choppy
StabilizationOn, at the highest level, for handheld and body mountsSmooths trail chatter without extra gear
Colour profileStandard or flat if you plan to colour gradeFlat profiles hold more highlight and shadow detail but need grading
Wind noise reductionOnHelps; it will not remove all noise

Settings names differ between cameras, so treat these as starting points and test before a big ride.

Angles and how to use them

POV

Chest and helmet

Puts the viewer on the trail. Use it for the main ride flow, not for the entire video.

Wide

Trail-side, static

Camera on a tripod low to the ground, rider passes through frame. Great for scale, jumps and corners.

Chase

Following a friend

Ride behind and slightly to the side of another rider, so the trail unfolds in front of both of you.

Detail

Close-ups

Tyres on dirt, a drivetrain, dust off a berm, hands on bars. Cheap, quick shots that make an edit feel rich.

Aerial

Drone

Reveals the landscape and your line through it. Only use where it is legal, safe and permitted.

Light and timing

  • Golden hours (early morning and late afternoon) give soft, warm, directional light.
  • Midday forest creates harsh dappled light that confuses exposure. Shoot open sections or accept the contrast and expose for the brightest areas.
  • Cloudy days are underrated: even, soft light, no harsh shadows, rich greens.
  • Dust and mud look dramatic when backlit by low sun.

Filming habits that save you in the edit

  • Shoot every feature twice: once clean, once with a different angle.
  • Hold static shots for at least 10 seconds so you have room to cut.
  • Film your approach to a feature and your reaction after it. These beats make the story.
  • Say the date, trail name and anything notable aloud into the camera for later reference.
  • Check the lens is clean before each run.

Ride-day shot checklist

Tick shots off as you capture them. Your ticks are saved in this browser only.

Story beats to aim for Setup (where and why), build-up (the climb or approach), payoff (the fun descent), and wind-down (the people and the ending). Even a 60-second clip works better with this shape.
